Guide

An app for putting together the trajectory of a planetary spacecraft with your own hands. The hard equations are hidden inside, so you build a trajectory just by laying out dates and destinations.

Start with Reading the screen and draw a single trajectory to Mars. After that there are five tutorials, each based on a spacecraft that actually flew. Every one can be reproduced with the real launch date, so you can see in numbers why it had to be that day. If you would rather see the finished article first, the app's “⋯” → “Load an example” loads the very trajectories built here.

What it can and cannot do

Can Cannot
Trajectories to planets and small bodies (asteroids, comets)
Swingbys (gravity alone, or topped up with a burn)
Deep-space burns, entering and leaving orbits, atmospheric entry
Pick a rocket and estimate the launch mass and the mass left
A map of departure and arrival dates (a porkchop plot)
Auto-tune, which tightens the current design without changing its shape 		Continuous thrust, as from an ion engine
(think of it as a single burn instead)
Orbits around the Moon or a planet's satellites
Fine effects such as solar radiation pressure or other planets' pull
Spacecraft design: attitude control, communications, power
One number to read above all “Mass left”, at the bottom right of the screen, is the score that matters. It is the mass the chosen rocket can launch, less the fuel spent on burns — the weight available for the spacecraft itself. The larger it is, the better the design.
